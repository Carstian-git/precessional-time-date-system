Human precessional time and Human precessional calendar

A 36-hour day in which every hour is 10°, so the day makes a full 360° circle. A year that begins with the December solstice. 13 months of 28 days. 12 eras that follow the precession of the equinoxes. Everything is computed from the real motions of the Sun, the Moon and the Earth's axis, with no religious tradition behind it.

Your time, on two watches

The same moment, read on two dials: the classic 12-hour watch and the 36-hour watch, in zone , estimated from your device’s time zone. You choose the exact place in Tools.

On the 36-hour watch, solar noon is at hour 18 and the short hand makes a single turn a day.

The 36-hour clock

An hour is 10°, a minute 1°, a second 0.1°. Solar noon is at hour 18, midnight at hour 0. One subsecond is exactly one standard second, so conversion needs no rounding.

The 13-month year

The year starts at the December solstice and has 13 months of 28 days, which is 52 whole weeks, plus one or two days outside the week.

Details

The 12 eras

The Earth's axis completes a cycle in about 25,772 years. We split it into 12 eras of 2,147 or 2,148 years. The current era, the 12th (ER 12 in codes), began in the year 293.

Why “precessional” and “human”

“Precessional” comes from the precession of the equinoxes, the slow motion of the Earth's axis that divides time into 12 eras. “Human” means at the scale of human perception: the system uses only what any person on Earth can observe, namely the Sun, the Moon and the sky. A single precessional cycle, the current one (23332 BCE – 2440 CE), covers all known human history, so any date in the past has a unique code. Read more about the name and the limits.
