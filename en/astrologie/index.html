Astrology in brief: what is computed and what is believed

Many people read their horoscope before leaving home. Some postpone a trip if the “angles” are bad. This tutorial explains, briefly and without mockery, what a horoscope computes, what “trine”, “square” or “Chiron” mean, and what has been tested so far. The computing part can be followed on the dials of the Planets and zodiac page.

What it is and what it is not

Astronomy measures where celestial bodies are. Astrology starts from the same positions and gives them meanings: character, opportunities, risks. The first part is calculation and can be checked; the second is interpretation. The two parted ways only in the modern age: the zodiac, as a system of reference points in the sky, and the horoscope, as an attempt to know what comes next, share the same ancient origin in Mesopotamia.

The four ingredients of a horoscope

  1. Signs. The tropical zodiac has 12 sectors of 30° on the ecliptic, starting at 0° Aries at the March equinox. They are grouped into four elements (fire: Aries, Leo, Sagittarius; earth: Taurus, Virgo, Capricorn; air: Gemini, Libra, Aquarius; water: Cancer, Scorpio, Pisces) and three modalities (cardinal: Aries, Cancer, Libra, Capricorn; fixed: Taurus, Leo, Scorpio, Aquarius; mutable: Gemini, Virgo, Sagittarius, Pisces).
  2. Planets. For astrologers the “planets” are the Sun and Moon (the luminaries), Mercury, Venus, Mars, Jupiter and Saturn, known since antiquity, plus Uranus (discovered 1781), Neptune (1846) and Pluto (1930, reclassified in 2006 as a dwarf planet but kept in horoscopes). What matters is which sign each one is in at the chosen moment.
  3. Time and place. A natal chart is made for the moment and place of birth. The exact time decides the ascendant, the sign rising on the eastern horizon (it changes about every 2 hours, i.e. 1° per 4 minutes), and the division of the sky into 12 “houses”. There are several house systems, and they do not agree.
  4. Aspects. These are angular distances between two bodies. The “major” ones, known since antiquity, are conjunction (0°), sextile (60°), square (90°), trine (120°) and opposition (180°). Tradition gives them meanings: trine and sextile are “harmonious”, square and opposition “tense”. The orb, the tolerance within which an angle counts as “exact”, differs from author to author.

Who is Chiron? Does astronomy mention it?

Yes. In astronomy, 2060 Chiron is a small body discovered on 1 November 1977 by Charles Kowal, the first of the class called “centaurs”: objects with orbits between Saturn and Uranus. Its orbit takes about 50 years, is very elongated (from about 8.5 AU at perihelion, in 1996, to about 19 AU) and its diameter is of the order of 200 km, so it is not a planet proper. It also carries a comet designation, 95P/Chiron, because it sometimes shows a coma. The name comes from Greek mythology: the centaur Chiron, teacher and healer of Achilles and Asclepius.

In astrology, Chiron was adopted after 1977 and is called “the wounded healer”: by interpretation, it stands for a wound that becomes the ability to help others. Because its orbit is irregular, it stays in a sign for a long or a short time: between about a year and a half and several years, depending on the region. Its aspects are read like those of any planet.

How to read a natal chart, step by step

  1. Convert the legal birth time to universal time (chapter 7 of the book: time zones, summer time).
  2. Compute each body’s ecliptic longitude at that moment (dial 1 of Planets and zodiac) and find its sign and degree.
  3. Compute the ascendant from the time and the latitude of the place.
  4. Measure the angles between bodies and keep those within the chosen orb (dial 3).
  5. Only now does interpretation begin: each body, sign and angle is given a meaning according to each astrologer’s tradition. Steps 1–4 are calculation, step 5 is not.

Where do newspaper and monthly horoscopes come from?

A newspaper cannot ask millions of readers for their birth time, so it keeps one element, the sign the Sun was in at birth (the sun sign). A monthly or daily horoscope then sets the planets’ positions during that period against that sign: the Moon changes sign every 2–3 days, the Sun, Mercury and Venus in almost a month, Mars in almost two, Jupiter in a year, Saturn in two and a half years. That is why Mercury’s retrogrades appear in horoscopes: they are a real phenomenon in the sky (an illusion of perspective, not a real turning back), to which interpretations are then attached. The paths can be followed on dial 2.

What has been tested

  • In a double-blind test published in Nature in 1985, Shawn Carlson asked astrologers to match natal charts with psychological profiles. The results were at chance level, that is, they showed no link between natal chart and character (after the study’s known summary; see the sources).
  • The Forer or Barnum effect: people given the same vague text, presented as personal, rated it on average as very fitting (around 4.3 out of 5 in Forer’s 1949 experiment, according to secondary sources). A horoscope written in general terms seems written for each reader.
  • Western zodiac signs are tropical, and precession shifts the equinox relative to the constellations by 1° every 71.6 years or so: the offset from the same-name constellations is about 24°, almost a whole sign. Indian astrology uses the sidereal zodiac and corrects the offset; Western astrology does not.
  • For astronomers there is no physical mechanism by which a distant planet would influence a person’s character at birth. The consensus of the scientific community is that astrology has no supporting evidence, and it calls it pseudoscience.

None of this means those who read horoscopes are naive: a prediction that seems to give us control over what we cannot control is appealing. A practical rule: to decide whether to go out, look at the weather, the traffic and how well we slept, not at the angle between two planets.
