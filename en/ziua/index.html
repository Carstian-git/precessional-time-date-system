Sunrise, sunset and lunar phases

 
 

The Sun rises and sets at times that depend on the place and the day of the year, and the Moon at times that slip by almost an hour a day. Here you see them for your place, on a day dial of 36 new hours (or 24 standard hours), with the twilight coloured, and you see what the Moon looks like on each day of the 28-day HPT month.

The day on a dial

The dial is the whole day, starting at the top with local midnight. The colours show the Sun: yellow, day; orange, civil twilight (Sun between 0.8° and 6° below the horizon); blue, nautical twilight; dark, night. The arrows ↑ ↓ are sunrise and sunset, ☉ is noon, and the ☽ circles on the inner ring are moonrise and moonset.

The sky above the horizon

The Sun (yellow) and the Moon (with its phase) against the horizon, azimuth across (N, E, S, W) and height up. The dotted lines are their path on the chosen day.

The Moon on the chosen day

The Moon's look is the same everywhere on Earth (only its orientation differs). The percentage is the lit part of the disc.

Lunar phases in the month:

Each day shows the Moon at mid-day. Days with a main phase have a yellow ring; press a day to choose it.

How to use the dials

  1. Choose the day, the place (latitude and longitude) and the time system. Bucharest, 44.43° N and 26.10° E, is filled in.
  2. In HPT the times are new hours (HN:MN) by the chosen zone; in standard time, by the UTC±h zone you type (3 in summer, 2 in winter in Romania).
  3. Drag the time slider: the hand moves on the dial and the heights of the Sun and the Moon change. Press a day in the Moon's month to choose it.
  4. Beyond the polar circles the Sun may not rise or may not set for a day or more.

What the page does not show

  • Sunrise and sunset are computed for the mathematical horizon with standard refraction (−0.833° for the Sun, +0.125° for the Moon); terrain and weather can shift the times by minutes.
  • For the Moon we use a mean distance; the real moonrise may differ by a few minutes.
  • The time zone and daylight saving time must be typed by you; the page does not guess them.
