How the Human precessional time (HPT) system works

Human precessional time (HPT) measures time as an angle and anchors the year and the eras to the real motions of the Sun, the Moon and the Earth's axis. The rules below come with examples. You can try them in the tools.

The 36-hour clock

The day has 36 new hours and equals one full 360° rotation. Solar noon is at hour 18, like “12” on a classic clock, and midnight is hour 0. One subsecond is exactly one SI second, so conversion with standard time is exact.

Quick rule: standard hours × 1.5 = new hours. Example: 15:30:45 is 55,845 seconds after midnight, so new hour 23, minute 2, second 6, subsecond 21, which is 232.6875°. It is written 23:02:06:21.

One degree of longitude is one new minute, so local mean time is UTC plus the longitude in new minutes. The 36 zones are 10° bands, and the offset from mean solar time is at most ±5 new minutes. There is no daylight saving time.

The year and the 13-month calendar

  • The year begins on the day of the December solstice. It has 365 days, or 366 in leap years (the year number is a multiple of 4 but not of 128). The mean year is 365.2421875 days, very close to the tropical year.
  • 13 months of 28 days, that is 364 days and exactly 52 weeks. Each month starts on Monday and ends on Sunday. Day 365 is the Year Day and in leap years day 366 is the Leap Day; both sit outside the week.
  • 73 solar arcs of 5 days each form a second count of the year.
  • Solar quadrants (1–4) are bounded by the real solstices and equinoxes, computed astronomically to better than a minute.
  • Moon phases are the real ones. Lunar day 1 is the local day of the astronomical new moon.

A year is named after the Gregorian year that contains 1 July of the new year. For example, year 2026 begins on 21.12.2025 and ends on 20.12.2026. The weekday in this calendar usually differs from the Gregorian one: 5 October 2026 is a Monday in the Gregorian calendar but a Tuesday (day 9 of month 11) in this one.

The 12 precessional eras

The Earth's axis traces a cycle of about 25,772 years, split into 12 precessional eras (ER 1–12) of 2,147 or 2,148 years. The year within an era is called AE and there is no year zero. The reference is the star Spica. With them all of history can be dated: 785 BCE = ER 11, AE 1072, and 2026 CE = ER 12, AE 1734. The eras are units of time and have nothing to do with astrology. The full code

The format is ER-AE-CS-AS.ZA-CL-LC-ZN HN:MN:SN:SS suffix. Example (5 October 2026, 12:00 UTC): 12-1734-4-58.4-25-11-09 21:00:00:00 F+03 means era 12 (Pisces), year 1734 of the era; solar quadrant 4, arc 58 day 4; lunar day 25; month 11, day 9; new time 21:00:00:00; zone +3.

For everyday use the short form is enough, for example ER 12 · AE 1734 · month 11 · day 9 · 21:00:00. Hour, minute and second are written with two digits, although minutes and seconds only run from 0 to 9.
